Agrarian Revolution

Meaning

The Agrarian Revolution refers to the major changes and improvementd in agriculture that increased food production and improved farming methods.

It mainly took place in Britain during the 17th and 18th centuries.

Causes of the Agrarian Revolution

    Population growth-Increased demand for food.

    New farming methods- Farmers introduced better ways of farming.

    Land enclosure-small pieces of land were combined into large farms.

    Scientific discoveries-new knowledge improved agriculture.

    Demand for raw materials-Industries needed agricultural products.

    Improved transport- Made it easier to transport farm products.

Major Inventions and improvements.

1.Seed Drill by Jethro TullHelped farmers plant seeds in straight rows and at the correct depth.

2.Crop Rotation by Charles TownshendGrowing different crops on the same land in different seasons to maintain soil ferility.

3.Selective Breeding by Robert Bakewellimproving the quality of livestock through controlled breeding.

4.Treshing MachineMade separating grain from stalks easier and faster.

Positive effects of agrarian revolution.

1.Increased food production.

2.Improved farming methods.

3.Increased population.

4.Growth OF Industries .

5.Creation of emproyment opportunities.

6.Expansion of internation trade.

7.Improved standards of living for some people.

Negative effects.

1.Unemployment among some farm workers.

2.Poor Farmers lost access to common land.

3.Growth of overcrowded towns.

4.Increased poverty among displaced rural families.

Importance of agrarian.

1.It improved agriculture productivity.

2.It encouraged mordern farming.

4.It supplies raw materials to industries.

Relationship between Agrarian and industrial revolution.

The Agrarian revolution contributed to the industrial revolution by:

-Providing food for factory.

-Supplying raw materials to industries.

-Releasing labour from agriculture to factories.

-Increasing wealth and investments.
